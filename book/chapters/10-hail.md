# Chapter 10: Hailstones the Size of Grapefruits

Hail forms when ice gets tossed up and down inside a storm, adding a layer each time. The bigger the updraft, the longer the ice stays aloft and the larger it grows. On the Plains and in northern Italy, that leads to some very large rocks.

1. **The biggest hailstone in U.S. history fell in Vivian, South Dakota.** On 23 July 2010 a stone measuring 8.0 inches (20.3 cm) across fell and weighed 1.94 pounds. Both numbers are U.S. records, confirmed by the National Climatic Extremes Committee.

2. **Europe's record is a 19-centimetre stone from Italy.** It fell at Azzano Decimo on 24 July 2023, breaking a record set only five days earlier when a 16-centimetre stone landed at Carmignano di Brenta.

3. **The heaviest stones on record fell in Bangladesh.** The World Meteorological Organization recognises stones of up to 1.02 kilograms (2.25 pounds) at Gopalganj on 14 April 1986. The storm is reported to have killed 92 people.

4. **Moradabad, India, had a deadly hailstorm in 1888.** On 30 April, stones reported to be as large as oranges fell during a severe storm. Records list more than 200 deaths, though the figures come from historical accounts.

5. **A Nebraska stone held the diameter record for seven years.** The hailstone at Aurora, Nebraska, on 22 June 2003 measured 7 inches (17.8 cm) across and held the U.S. record until Vivian beat it in 2010.

6. **Hailstones have rings like trees.** Cut one open and you'll see alternating clear and cloudy layers. Researchers read them as a record of the stone's trips through different parts of the storm.

7. **Big stones fall fast.** Baseball-sized hail drops at around 100 miles an hour. That speed, not the weight alone, is why cars and roofs take such punishment.

8. **Calgary's 2020 hailstorm was the costliest in Canadian history.** The storm on 13 June 2020 caused insured losses of about C$1.2 billion, mostly to cars and roofs in the city's northeast.

9. **Denver's hailstorm of 1990 caused heavy insured damage too.** The storm on 11 July 1990 produced losses of about $625 million, making it one of the costliest in the United States at that time.

10. **Hail alley covers the High Plains.** The corner where Colorado, Wyoming, and Nebraska meet sees more hail days than almost anywhere in North America because the storms form where cold air aloft meets moist, hot air at the surface.

11. **Guadalajara was buried in hail in June 2019.** An intense storm on 30 June dropped hail that piled up in drifts reported as deep as 1.5 metres in parts of the city. Residents shovelled it off their streets as if it were snow, and the hot-weather city photographed cars half buried.

12. **Hail grows in a cold band of the storm.** The stone gains most of its mass between roughly −10°C and −30°C, where supercooled droplets freeze on contact and add a layer.

13. **A supercell's updraft can exceed 100 miles an hour.** Meteorologists estimate that the largest stones need updrafts of about that speed or more to stay aloft long enough to reach their size.

14. **Hail often falls in narrow swaths.** A storm may flatten crops along a strip a mile wide and leave the next farm untouched. Insurance adjusters and weather services map them with radar and ground reports.

15. **Radar can estimate hail size, with limits.** Dual-polarisation radar measures the shapes and sizes of particles. It tells forecasters where the large stones likely are, but measuring in the field remains the best check.

16. **Hail is not frozen rain.** Frozen rain is sleet, small clear pellets. Hail forms only in thunderstorms and grows from layering. Graupel, a softer snowy pellet, is its smaller relative.

17. **Hail can be piled up by rain and wind.** A mass of melting hailstones can look like snow cover, and in extreme cases flows like slush into drainage systems, blocking them and causing flash flooding.

18. **Hail suppression is controversial.** Programs in places like Russia, Canada, and France have seeded storms with silver iodide to try to reduce hail. Scientists still debate whether it works, and the evidence is mixed, so any claim about effectiveness remains **[UNCONFIRMED]**.

19. **Europe's hail season is growing worse.** European researchers have reported rising hail damage trends in recent years, with 2023 setting multiple records for stone size. Scientists are still working out how much of that is climate change and how much is natural variability.

20. **Insurers treat hail as a major peril.** In the United States, hail causes billions of dollars in damage to roofs and vehicles in an average year, and some regions require special roofing material.
