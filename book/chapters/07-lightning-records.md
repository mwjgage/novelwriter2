# Chapter 7: Lightning Strikes That Broke the Record Books

Lightning is quick, so the records are measured in kilometres and fractions of a second. Most of what's here was found by satellite or high-speed camera. A few records were found years after the fact, in data nobody had looked at closely.

1. **The longest lightning flash ever recorded covered 829 kilometres.** The World Meteorological Organization certified it in 2025. It happened in October 2017, stretching from eastern Texas to near Kansas City. Nobody noticed at the time; researchers found it by re-examining satellite data.

2. **That is about the distance from Paris to Venice.** The flash beat the previous record by 61 kilometres, which was the 768-kilometre flash across Mississippi, Louisiana, and Texas on 29 April 2020.

3. **Before that, the record was 709 kilometres in Brazil.** It occurred on 31 October 2018 and held until the 2020 flash passed it.

4. **A single flash lasted more than 17 seconds.** On 18 June 2020, a flash over Uruguay and northern Argentina ran for 17.102 seconds, the longest-duration lightning flash the WMO has certified.

5. **Roy Sullivan was struck by lightning seven times.** The Virginia park ranger survived strikes between 1942 and 1977, and Guinness lists him as the person hit most often and surviving all of them.

6. **One lake in Venezuela has lightning on roughly 260 nights a year.** The Catatumbo lightning, over the mouth of the Catatumbo River at Lake Maracaibo, produces near-continuous storms in the evening. Satellite data show it among the most lightning-dense places on Earth. In early 2010 it paused for several weeks during a drought and then resumed.

7. **A bolt is hotter than the surface of the Sun.** The channel heats the air to roughly 30,000°C, about five times hotter than the Sun's surface, though the bolt is only a few centimetres wide.

8. **Earth gets around 40 to 50 lightning flashes every second.** That adds up to over a billion flashes a year, counting those inside clouds.

9. **Positive lightning is the "bolt from the blue."** These strikes carry a positive charge from the top of a storm cloud and can hit the ground tens of kilometres from the storm. They tend to be stronger and longer than ordinary negative strikes.

10. **High-speed cameras show a bolt building downward in steps.** B.F.J. Schonland's team in South Africa recorded the stepped leader in the 1930s using a special camera. Modern cameras run at hundreds of thousands of frames per second and show the leader branching toward the ground.

11. **The Empire State Building is hit about 20 to 25 times a year.** Tall structures act as lightning rods. The building's rod has done its job since 1931.

12. **A French experiment proved Franklin's idea in 1752.** Thomas-François Dalibard erected a tall iron rod at Marly-la-Ville and drew sparks from a passing storm on 10 May 1752, a month before Franklin's kite experiment.

13. **Lightning melts sand into glass tubes.** Fulgurites form when a strike fuses silica. Some run for more than a metre underground and branch like roots.

14. **Lichtenberg figures can appear on skin after a strike.** The red, fern-shaped marks, sometimes called lightning flowers, fade after hours or days and aren't burns.

15. **Thunder is a shockwave.** The superheated air expands faster than sound. Count the seconds between flash and bang and divide by three to estimate the distance in kilometres.

16. **Lightning kills about 20 people a year in the United States.** That's the recent annual average according to the National Weather Service, far lower than in past decades thanks to better shelter and warnings.

17. **Florida's Lightning Alley is the busiest in the U.S.** The corridor between Tampa and Orlando gets the highest density of strikes in the country because sea breezes from both coasts collide there each afternoon.

18. **Lightning helps fertilise the planet.** The heat of a bolt splits nitrogen molecules in the air, which form compounds that rain carries to the ground, where plants can use them.

19. **Satellites now watch lightning continuously.** NOAA's GOES-16, launched in 2016, carries the Geostationary Lightning Mapper, which records flashes across the Americas day and night. Much of the megaflash record work relied on it.

20. **A single bolt killed 21 people in 1975.** Guinness lists a strike on a hut in Manica, Zimbabwe on 23 December 1975 as the deadliest single lightning strike on record.

21. **Planes get struck about once a year.** Commercial aircraft are designed to carry the current around the passengers and out the wingtips and tail, and the strikes usually leave only small burn marks.
