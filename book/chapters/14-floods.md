# Chapter 14: Rain Records and Floods That Rewrote the Map

An inch of rain on a lawn is a nuisance. Eighty inches on a mountain is a record. Flash floods turn dry streets into rivers in minutes, and the footage is often shot by people standing too close.

1. **Foc-Foc, Réunion, holds the 24-hour rainfall record: 1,825 millimetres.** The WMO recognises the reading from 7 and 8 January 1966, during Tropical Cyclone Denise. That's about 72 inches in a day, on an island with mountains over 3,000 metres high that force the moist air upward.

2. **Cherrapunji set the 12-month record in the 1860s.** The Indian hill town recorded about 26,470 millimetres between August 1860 and July 1861, which is more than 86 feet of rain.

3. **Mawsynram and Cherrapunji argue over "wettest."** Both lie in Meghalaya, India, and they trade the title depending on how the average is calculated. Both receive well over 10,000 millimetres in an average year.

4. **A minute of rain in Maryland set a record.** At Unionville on 4 July 1956, 31.2 millimetres (1.23 inches) fell in one minute, the highest rate recognised by the WMO.

5. **Hurricane Harvey dropped over 60 inches on parts of Texas.** In August 2017, the storm stalled over southeastern Texas and produced the highest rainfall total from a tropical cyclone in the continental United States.

6. **Zhengzhou received 201.9 millimetres in a single hour.** On 20 July 2021, the Chinese city recorded the highest hourly figure since measurements began in 1951. Fourteen people died when subway Line 5 flooded, and the province's death toll was reported in the hundreds.

7. **A Colorado canyon flood killed 144 people.** On 31 July 1976, about 12 inches of rain fell in roughly four hours over the Big Thompson River, and a wall of water swept down the canyon during a holiday weekend.

8. **The United Arab Emirates had its heaviest rain since records began in 1949.** On 16 April 2024, a persistent storm system dropped 254.8 millimetres in 24 hours at Khatm Al Shakla, near Al Ain, and 144 millimetres at Dubai International Airport. Cloud seeding was rumoured as a cause, but experts pointed to the storm system itself and warm seas.

9. **Pakistan's 2022 floods covered about a third of the country.** Monsoon rains starting in June brought record totals in Sindh and Balochistan. The government reported around 1,700 deaths, and tens of millions of people were affected.

10. **Storm Daniel's floods destroyed parts of Derna, Libya.** In September 2023, two dams upstream failed after extreme rainfall and swept through the city. Thousands died.

11. **An atmospheric river can carry a Mississippi's worth of water.** NOAA says the average one transports an amount of water vapour equal to 7.5 to 15 times the average flow at the mouth of the Mississippi River.

12. **Atmospheric rivers now have a rating scale.** In 2019, Scripps scientists introduced a five-category scale, from beneficial to hazardous, based on how much moisture they carry and how long they last.

13. **The 1927 Mississippi flood inundated about 27,000 square miles.** The Great Mississippi Flood displaced hundreds of thousands of people and led to federal flood-control legislation.

14. **Mount Waiʻaleʻale on Kauai receives more than 400 inches a year.** It's one of the wettest spots on Earth, soaked by trade winds that hit the mountain from the northeast.

15. **Flash floods are the biggest weather killers in some years.** In the United States, they've often ranked among the top causes of weather-related fatalities, and a large fraction of the deaths occur in vehicles driven into water.

16. **Six inches of moving water can knock you down.** A foot can float a car. The National Weather Service uses the slogan "Turn Around, Don't Drown."

17. **Floods can make desert lakes appear.** After exceptional rain, Australia's Lake Eyre fills and draws birds from across the continent. The basin's rivers are far from the sea, and it can take months for water to arrive.

18. **A 2015 Atacama deluge hit the driest desert.** In March 2015, heavy rain in northern Chile caused floods and mudslides in towns that normally see almost no rain.

19. **A monsoon is a seasonal wind reversal.** The word comes from the Arabic *mawsim*, meaning season. South Asia gets most of its yearly rain in a few months.

20. **Cameras caught the Zhengzhou subway.** Video from inside train cars showed water at passengers' chests, and the clips triggered a national debate about urban drainage and preparedness.
