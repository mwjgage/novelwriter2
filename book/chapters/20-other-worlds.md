# Chapter 20: Weather on Other Worlds

Earth's weather runs on water. Elsewhere in the solar system it runs on methane, sulfuric acid, carbon dioxide, and a few things nobody has seen directly. Spacecraft cameras have sent back the proof, and telescopes are starting to read the skies of planets around other stars.

1. **Jupiter's Great Red Spot is a storm wider than Earth.** It's an anticyclone that has been observed for at least 150 years, and it has been shrinking. It rotates counterclockwise, completing a turn in roughly six days, and winds along its edge exceed 400 km/h.

2. **Juno flew over it in 2017.** On 10 July 2017, NASA's Juno spacecraft passed about 9,000 kilometres above the storm's cloud tops and returned close-up images.

3. **Saturn has a six-sided jet stream.** The hexagon sits around the north pole, roughly 30,000 kilometres across, with each side about 14,500 kilometres long. Voyager 1 found it in 1980 and Cassini photographed it in detail beginning in 2004. Its winds blow at about 320 km/h.

4. **Saturn gets a giant storm about every 30 years.** The Great White Spot that erupted in 2010 wrapped around the planet and was tracked by Cassini.

5. **Venus has clouds of sulfuric acid.** The planet's surface temperature is about 465°C, and acid droplets in the clouds evaporate well above the ground, so the rain never lands.

6. **Venus's atmosphere rotates faster than the planet.** The cloud tops circle the planet in about four Earth days, while Venus takes 243 days to rotate once on its axis.

7. **Lightning on Venus is debated.** Past missions gave conflicting hints. In 2021, NASA's Parker Solar Probe detected radio whistler waves during a flyby that were consistent with lightning. The claim remains **[UNCONFIRMED]** until a direct optical observation.

8. **Jupiter has lightning, first seen by Voyager 1 in 1979.** Juno has since recorded it in detail, including flashes at the poles.

9. **Juno's data point to "mushballs."** Researchers proposed in 2020 that ammonia and water combine to form slushy hail deep in Jupiter's atmosphere. It's a **[THEORY]** based on indirect measurements.

10. **Mars has dust devils and global dust storms.** Rovers have photographed dust devils crossing the plains, and a storm in 2018 covered the whole planet (see Chapter 11).

11. **Mars has snow, but it evaporates before it lands.** NASA's Phoenix lander's laser radar saw ice crystals falling from clouds in 2008 that vaporised before reaching the ground.

12. **Titan has methane rain.** Saturn's largest moon has clouds, rivers, and lakes of liquid methane and ethane. The Huygens probe landed there on 14 January 2005, and Cassini mapped the lakes.

13. **Neptune has the fastest winds in the solar system.** Winds reach about 2,000 km/h (1,200 mph), roughly five times stronger than anything recorded on Earth. Voyager 2 measured them in 1989.

14. **Neptune's Great Dark Spot vanished.** Voyager 2 discovered it in 1989, and when the Hubble Space Telescope looked in 1994 it was gone. New dark spots have appeared since.

15. **Diamond rain on Neptune and Uranus is a [THEORY].** Laboratory experiments in 2017 showed that extreme pressure could squeeze hydrocarbons into diamond. Whether it actually rains down inside those planets hasn't been observed.

16. **Pluto has blue haze.** NASA's New Horizons saw layers of haze rising roughly 200 kilometres above the surface in 2015, with a blue tint.

17. **An exoplanet has winds of 8,700 km/h.** Astronomers measured the flow on HD 189733b, blowing from the day side to the night side, in 2015. It was the first time weather on an extrasolar planet was mapped directly.

18. **That planet may be blue because of glass.** Hubble observations in 2013 showed HD 189733b has a blue colour, probably from silicate particles in its clouds. The idea of "glass rain" is an inference rather than an observation, so treat it as a **[THEORY]**.

19. **WASP-76b may have iron rain.** In 2020, astronomers reported iron vapour on the evening edge of the planet's day side, which they interpreted as iron condensing on the night side. That interpretation is still **[THEORY]**.

20. **The Sun has its own weather.** Coronal mass ejections, flares, and the solar wind affect every planet, as Chapter 13 describes. Earth's weather is just the small part we can feel.
