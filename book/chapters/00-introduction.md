# Introduction: Please Hold, the Sky Is Busy

In 1884, a photographer in Kansas pointed a camera at a tornado and made one of the earliest known photographs of one. Weather photography has been a risky hobby ever since. Cameras are cheaper now and they live in every pocket, car dashboard, doorbell, and satellite. Almost anything the atmosphere does gets recorded by somebody. The result is a visual record of a planet that regularly forgets it is supposed to be calm.

This book collects the strangest of it: hundreds of facts about rare clouds, hurricane eyes, fire whirls, snow that rolls itself into cylinders, lightning that crosses state lines, and storms on planets where the word "weather" gets a lot of extra zeros attached. Each chapter has a theme and between twenty and twenty-three facts. You can read straight through or open the book anywhere and start.

## A few ground rules

Every fact in here is meant to be true. Where a number is a record, the book says who certified it. Where a record is disputed, you'll be told that too. If something is a theory, a legend, or an unverified claim, it carries a label:

- **[THEORY]** means scientists have a proposed explanation that isn't settled.
- **[MYTH]** means a popular belief the evidence doesn't support.
- **[UNCONFIRMED]** means it was reported, but nobody has verified it to the standard of a record.
- **[DISPUTED]** and **[DEBATED]** mean experts disagree about the measurement or the finding.
- **[UNOFFICIAL]** means a proposal that no governing body has adopted.

Weather records change. Researchers re-examine old storms and new instruments find old bolts. A famous example is lightning: in 2025 the World Meteorological Organization certified a flash from 2017 that nobody noticed at the time, because the data sat unexamined for years. Figures here are current as of writing. If a record matters to you, check the issuing agency.

Last thing. Please don't chase any of this on foot. The people who got these shots usually had training, equipment, and a good deal of luck. The facts will keep until you get indoors.
