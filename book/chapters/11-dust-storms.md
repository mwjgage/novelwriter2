# Chapter 11: Walls of Dust and Sandstorms From Hell

A thunderstorm's cold downdraft can scoop up desert soil and push it ahead as a wall. A dry year on a plowed prairie can send the entire topsoil east. And the Sahara quietly feeds the Amazon.

1. **"Haboob" comes from Arabic.** The word comes from *habūb*, meaning blasting or drifting, and describes a dust wall pushed out by a thunderstorm. It's most associated with Sudan, Arabia, and the American Southwest.

2. **A dust wall over Phoenix on 5 July 2011 rose over a mile high.** The storm rolled across the city in the evening, cut visibility to near zero, and cut power to thousands. Photos and time-lapse videos spread worldwide.

3. **A haboob is a thunderstorm's cold outflow.** When rain falls into dry air and evaporates, the air cools and sinks, then spreads out along the ground as a gust front carrying dust before it.

4. **Black Sunday hit the Plains on 14 April 1935.** A huge dust storm swept across Oklahoma, Texas, Kansas, and neighbouring states in the afternoon. Witnesses described sudden darkness and static electricity strong enough to stall cars.

5. **A reporter named the Dust Bowl the day after.** Robert Geiger of the Associated Press used the phrase "Dust Bowl" in a dispatch after Black Sunday, and it stuck as the name for the region and the era.

6. **Dust from the Plains fell on Washington.** In May 1934, a large storm carried topsoil east. Dust fell on cities from Chicago to New York and was reported on ships hundreds of miles out in the Atlantic.

7. **The Sahara fertilises the Amazon.** NASA satellite data show about 182 million tons of dust leaving the Sahara in a year, with about 27.7 million tons settling over the Amazon basin. It brings roughly 22,000 tons of phosphorus, a nutrient that rain forest soils lose.

8. **The Bodélé Depression in Chad is the planet's biggest single dust source.** The dry lake bed, the remains of ancient Lake Mega-Chad, supplies a large share of the dust that travels west.

9. **"Godzilla" crossed the Atlantic in June 2020.** An exceptional Saharan dust plume reached the Caribbean and the southern United States. Air quality in parts of the Caribbean was described as the worst in decades.

10. **The 2018 Martian dust storm silenced a rover.** A dust storm that grew to cover the whole planet in mid-2018 blocked sunlight from the solar panels of NASA's Opportunity rover. Contact was lost on 10 June 2018, and NASA declared the mission over in February 2019.

11. **Dust devils clean Martian solar panels.** Mars rovers Spirit and Opportunity lasted longer than planned partly because wind events, including dust devils, blew dust off their panels. Cameras have photographed dust devils crossing the Martian surface many times.

12. **Perseverance filmed dust devils too.** The rover's cameras and weather sensors have recorded them passing near Jezero Crater, giving scientists data on their size and the pressure drops they cause.

13. **Dust darkens mountain snow.** In Colorado's Rocky Mountains, dust blown from desert regions has been shown to speed up spring snowmelt by a few weeks, which affects the river flows downstream.

14. **Valley fever travels in the dust.** The fungus *Coccidioides* lives in soils of the American Southwest, and storms can lift its spores. Public health officials in Arizona and California warn about it after major dust events.

15. **Beijing's worst storm in a decade came in March 2021.** Dust from the Gobi Desert turned the city's sky orange and pushed air quality readings beyond the top of standard scales.

16. **Iraq endured a string of dust storms in spring 2022.** Several events in April and May sent thousands of people to hospital with breathing problems and closed airports. Drought and land degradation have made them more frequent.

17. **Shrinking lakes feed the dust.** The Aral Sea in Central Asia lost most of its water in the twentieth century, leaving a dry bed whose salty dust is carried by wind. Owens Lake in California was a similar source before the city of Los Angeles began controlling it.

18. **Dust storms cause highway pile-ups.** Sudden zero visibility on interstates in Arizona, New Mexico, and Texas has caused multi-vehicle crashes. Transportation departments there advise drivers to pull off the road and turn off their lights.

19. **A tall dust devil can reach a kilometre or more.** On Earth, they usually last minutes. On Mars, some towers rise to several kilometres.

20. **Sand dunes can sing.** Some dune faces avalanche and make a low booming sound audible from far away. Marco Polo wrote of the "voices of spirits" in the deserts of Central Asia, and the effect is now studied by physicists, though the exact mechanism is still debated.
