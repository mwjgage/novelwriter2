# Chapter 13: When the Sun Hits Earth With a Light Show

Space weather isn't weather in the usual sense, but it hits the same instruments, and people point their cameras at it. When the Sun throws a large burst of plasma at Earth, the sky lights up and some of our hardware fails.

1. **The Carrington Event of 1859 is the strongest storm on record.** On 1 September, astronomer Richard Carrington saw a white-light flare on the Sun. A geomagnetic storm arrived within about a day, and auroras were reported as far south as the Caribbean.

2. **Telegraphs sparked and some kept running with no power.** Operators in North America and Europe reported shocks and fires at their equipment. Some sent messages for a while using only the current induced by the storm.

3. **A 1989 storm blacked out Quebec.** On 13 March, a geomagnetic storm collapsed Hydro-Québec's grid in about 90 seconds. Six million people were without power for around nine hours.

4. **The Halloween storms of 2003 darkened part of Sweden.** In late October, a series of strong flares and ejections caused a blackout in Malmö and damaged or disrupted satellites. Airlines rerouted polar flights.

5. **May 2024 brought the strongest storm in two decades.** On 10 and 11 May, a G5 geomagnetic storm, the top of NOAA's scale and the first since 2003, lit auroras as far south as Florida and Mexico. Some farm GPS systems in the U.S. Midwest were disrupted.

6. **The Sun's activity cycle peaked around 2024.** NASA and NOAA said in October 2024 that the Sun had reached the maximum period of Solar Cycle 25. Aurora sightings at low latitudes rose along with it.

7. **An X28 flare in 2003 may be the largest ever measured.** The flare on 4 November 2003 saturated the detectors, so its strength is an estimate, about X28.

8. **A near miss happened in 2012.** On 23 July 2012, a massive coronal mass ejection crossed Earth's orbit nine days before Earth got there. NASA's STEREO-A spacecraft recorded it. Researchers estimate it was comparable to the Carrington Event.

9. **A magnetic storm killed Starlink satellites in 2022.** SpaceX launched 49 satellites on 3 February 2022, and a geomagnetic storm increased drag in the upper atmosphere. About 38 of them were lost.

10. **A 1972 storm set off U.S. Navy sea mines.** On 4 August 1972, magnetic mines near Hải Phòng in North Vietnam detonated unexpectedly. Researchers later connected the explosions to a large geomagnetic storm.

11. **Green aurora comes from oxygen about 100 to 150 kilometres up.** Red appears higher, above about 200 kilometres, and blue or purple edges come from nitrogen.

12. **Aurora hunters named a new light "Steve."** Photographers in Alberta noticed a narrow purple ribbon in 2016 and joked about the name, borrowed from the film *Over the Hedge*. Scientists later turned it into a backronym: Strong Thermal Emission Velocity Enhancement.

13. **STEVE sometimes comes with a "picket fence."** The green streaks that hang beneath the purple band were identified in 2018 as a separate effect, though what produces them is still being studied.

14. **Aurora might make sound.** In 2016, Finnish researcher Unto Laine's team recorded crackling noises that appeared linked to auroral activity, apparently generated about 70 metres above the ground near a temperature inversion. The proposed mechanism is a **[THEORY]**.

15. **Auroras happen on other planets.** Hubble and the Juno spacecraft have imaged powerful auroras at Jupiter's poles, and Saturn has its own.

16. **Astronauts look down on the aurora.** The International Space Station orbits at about 400 kilometres, above the glow, which sits roughly 100 to 300 kilometres up. Crews photograph the curtains from above, sometimes with the station flying through the edge of them.

17. **Aurora can be seen from both poles at the same time.** The Northern and Southern lights often mirror one another in shape. Satellites have imaged both ovals at once.

18. **Solar wind travels at about 400 kilometres a second.** During storms it can be double that or more. A fast coronal mass ejection can cross the distance to Earth in less than a day.

19. **Citizen scientists track storms.** Projects like Aurorasaurus ask people to report sightings, and amateur photos helped identify STEVE.

20. **Satellite operators raise orbits during storms.** Heating of the upper atmosphere makes it expand, increasing drag on low satellites. Agencies issue warnings on NOAA's five-point scales for geomagnetic, solar radiation, and radio blackout storms.
